# Decision log

1. **AppleSupport as the first brand** — Apple has a large, recognizable support presence in this corpus, making a single-brand evaluation easier to explain.
2. **Broad 8-intent taxonomy** — reduces label ambiguity compared with dozens of micro-intents.
3. **Human-labelled gold set** — prevents circular evaluation from weak labels generated by the same model.
4. **TF-IDF + logistic regression** — fast, interpretable, reproducible baseline for a 15-minute reproduction target.
5. **TF-IDF retrieval** — makes historical evidence inspectable rather than hiding grounding inside embeddings.
6. **Top-5 evidence** — gives the evaluator multiple historical examples without overwhelming the UI.
7. **Conservative escalation** — uncertain cases should not be forced into auto-handling.
8. **Sensitive-pattern escalation** — account secrets and financial credentials should not be handled by a generic reply generator.
9. **Low-similarity escalation** — novelty is a reason to ask a human rather than hallucinate a resolution.
10. **Historical-response grounding** — the prototype does not invent refunds, account changes, credits, or policy exceptions.
11. **Majority baseline** — tests whether the classifier actually beats a trivial predictor.
12. **Nearest-neighbour baseline** — tests whether learned intent classification adds value beyond lexical similarity.
13. **LLM judge is secondary** — judge scores are evidence about response quality, not ground truth.
14. **Human agreement check** — a judge is only useful if its scores agree reasonably with humans on a shared sample.
15. **No deployment claim** — offline historical Twitter data cannot prove safety on today's live customer traffic.
